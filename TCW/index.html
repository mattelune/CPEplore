Beyond the Coding:

Investigating the Impact of Technology Use on Academic Performance Among First-Year Computer Engineering Students.

01 / Introduction

The digital shift in engineering education.

The New Normal in Philippine Higher Education. Philippine colleges and universities have fully embraced hybrid and blended learning. This shift gives students greater flexibility and access to digital learning resources. However, it also highlights persistent challenges such as unstable internet connections, limited access to devices, and unequal technological resources. These barriers continue to influence how effectively students participate in modern learning environments (Espinosa et al., 2023).

The Rise of AI and the Attention Dilemma. Today's college students are learning in an era shaped by Generative AI tools such as ChatGPT. These platforms can support research, problem-solving, and content creation, making academic tasks more efficient. At the same time, concerns about overreliance, academic integrity, and the weakening of critical thinking skills have emerged when AI is used irresponsibly (Hortelano & Salamia, 2025).

Technology has become a double-edged sword in higher education. It can enhance learning when used for academic purposes such as research, programming, and collaboration, but it can also become a source of distraction through excessive gaming, social media use, and online entertainment. As a result, the impact of technology depends largely on how students use it (Rizada & Rey, 2023).

Technology is now part of the everyday learning experience.

02 / Background of the Study

Navigating challenges among Computer Engineering students.

For first-year Computer Engineering students, technology is more than an additional learning resource. It is part of how they study, communicate, solve problems, and adjust to college life. Understanding its influence means looking beyond access and examining how students actually use it.

01 ⌘

The Computer Engineering Experience

Modern Computer Engineering education relies heavily on the integration of digital technologies in teaching and learning. Computer Engineering students regularly use programming platforms, digital learning systems, simulations, and online collaboration tools as part of their academic training.

However, first-year CpE students face a significant transition as they move from high school to a rigorous, technology-driven college environment. Many freshmen struggle with workload management, independent learning, and adapting to hybrid learning setups while meeting the demands of engineering education (Firmante, 2024).

Freshmen are a particularly important group to study because they are still developing the study habits, digital literacy skills, and time-management strategies needed to succeed in a technology-rich academic environment.

02 ◎

Access Is Not Enough

While access to laptops, smartphones, and internet connectivity is important, it does not automatically lead to academic success. Research suggests that students perform better when they possess strong self-regulation skills, digital literacy, and confidence in using technology effectively. What matters most is not simply having access to technology, but knowing how to use it purposefully and responsibly (Briones et al., 2023; Gayrama, 2025).

03 ⌁

The Core Challenge

This study investigates a contemporary challenge faced by first-year Computer Engineering students: How does the frequency and purpose of technology use affect academic performance?

Specifically, the study examines whether students use technology primarily for productive academic activities such as programming, online research, note-taking, and AI-assisted learning, or for non-academic activities such as social media, gaming, and entertainment. It then explores how these technology-use patterns may influence academic outcomes and overall student success.

04 ✳

Why This Matters

Understanding how technology influences academic performance can help students develop healthier and more productive digital habits. The findings may also assist educators and university administrators in designing programs and policies that strengthen digital literacy, encourage responsible technology use, and support the academic success and well-being of first-year Computer Engineering students.

03 / Research Overview

Understanding the bigger picture.

Technology use is not limited to a single activity. Students move between academic and nonacademic digital environments throughout the day. This study looks at three dimensions of that experience.

01

Technology Utilization

How often students use digital devices, platforms, applications, and AI tools in their daily routines.

02

Purpose of Use

How students divide their technology use between academic activities and nonacademic activities.

03

Academic Performance

How students' technology-use patterns relate to their academic tasks, learning experiences, and performance.

04 / Discussion

The many sides of technology use.

Technology plays an important role in higher education, especially in the academic performance of students by providing access to information, communication, and learning resources. However, the use of such technology can also bring challenges that may affect students' academic performance. The following discussions examine how technology is used in learning and how it affects students, particularly in their academic activities and performance.

01 / Academic Performance

Technology Use and Academic Performance

Kuş (2025), through a meta-analysis involving 124,166 students from 28 countries, found a small but significant negative association between technology-related activities and academic performance.

In contrast, Rizada and Rey (2023) demonstrated that technology-assisted instruction can improve students' mathematics performance, showing how technology can support learning when used properly and effectively.

The contradiction may have come from how technology was used. Kuş (2025) focused on general technology activities, which may include distractions and non-academic use. Rizada and Rey (2023), on the other hand, focused on structured, teacher-guided use. It suggests that the purpose and quality of use matters more than the amount of technology used.

02 / Learning Environment

Adjustment to Hybrid Learning

Firmante (2024) studied the experiences of Filipino first-year engineering students in adjusting to post-pandemic hybrid learning. It emphasized the point that students' academic success is influenced by their ability to adjust to hybrid learning, particularly in terms of self-management and social support.

03 / Digital Access

Technology Access and Institutional Support

Espinosa et al. (2023) identified poor internet connectivity, unequal access to technology, and limited institutional readiness as major challenges in Philippine education. These findings show that not all students have the same access to learning resources, which may affect how students complete their academic tasks and participate in technology-based learning.

Source: Pinterest
04 / Student Agency

Self-Regulation and Digital Literacy

Briones et al. (2023) found that students had moderate engagement and good self-regulation skills. The study explains how student success in online learning depends on how well they manage their own learning. It highlighted the importance of student engagement, self-regulation, and self-efficacy in online learning to achieve better learning outcomes.

Likewise, Gayrama (2025) found that motivation and self-regulation contribute to students' confidence in using educational technology. The findings demonstrate the benefits of digital literacy when it comes to student success.

Overall, the studies of Briones et al. (2023) and Gayrama (2025) show that the academic performance of students relies more on how the students are able to manage themselves when it comes to technology-based learning.

05 / Artificial Intelligence

Risks of AI in Higher Education

Hortelano and Salamia (2025) identified both the benefits and risks of artificial intelligence in higher education, including concerns about overreliance and misinformation. Their findings showed that students saw AI as a useful tool for learning, but they were also concerned about relying too much on it and receiving inaccurate information.

Source: pia.gov.ph
06 / Overall Perspective

Conclusion

Taking everything into consideration, technology can either help or hinder the academic performance of students. They may only really benefit from technology when its use is guided and purposeful. Moreover, their success relies on self-regulation, digital literacy, and confidence in using such technology.

06 / Significance of the Study

Why this research matters.

This study is significant because it aims to determine how the use of technology influences the academic performance of first-year Computer Engineering students. As technology becomes increasingly integrated into education, understanding its benefits and challenges is essential in promoting effective learning practices.

According to Kuş (2025), a meta-analysis involving 124,166 students from 28 countries found a small but statistically significant negative association between technology-related behaviors, such as smartphone use, social media use, and video gaming, and academic performance. Meanwhile, Viloan et al. (2024) emphasized that digital technologies can support students' learning by providing access to information, educational resources, and opportunities for communication and collaboration. Therefore, this study may provide valuable insights into how students' use of technology relates to their academic achievement.

Specifically, this study will benefit the following:

01

Students

This study will help first-year Computer Engineering students understand how their use of technology may influence their academic performance. It may encourage them to develop responsible technology habits, manage their screen time, minimize digital distractions, and maximize the use of technological resources for academic purposes.

02

Teachers and Instructors

The findings may provide teachers and instructors with a better understanding of how technology affects students' learning experiences and academic performance. This may help them develop appropriate teaching strategies and encourage the productive use of digital tools in classroom activities.

03

School Administrators

This study may serve as a reference for school administrators in developing policies, programs, and initiatives that promote responsible and effective technology use in the academic environment. It may also help identify areas where students need additional technological support and guidance.

04

Future Researchers

This study may serve as a reference for future researchers who wish to explore the relationship between technology use and academic performance. It may also provide a foundation for further investigations involving other academic programs, year levels, institutions, and factors affecting students' educational achievement.
